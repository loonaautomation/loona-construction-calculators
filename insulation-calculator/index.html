🌡️ Insulation Calculator

Insulating a loft, ceiling or wall: enter the area and the R-value you are aiming for, choose batts/rolls or blown-in, and get how many packs or bags to buy — plus the approximate thickness that reaches your target for the material you pick.

How to use the Insulation Calculator

  1. Choose metric or imperial units.
  2. Enter the length and width of the area (for a loft, the ceiling below it).
  3. Enter your target: R-value in imperial, RSI in metric (R-20 ≈ RSI 3.5; R-30 ≈ RSI 5.3).
  4. Pick the material and whether it comes as batts/rolls or blown-in bags.
  5. Copy the coverage per pack/bag for your target R from the product chart, then press Calculate.
Example: A 10 m × 5 m ceiling (50 m²) with packs covering 5 m² each needs 10 packs — 11 with the default 10% extra for cuts and joist gaps.

Frequently Asked Questions

What R-value do I need?

It depends on the element and your climate/building code. As a rough guide: uninsulated loft ceilings are often brought to R-30 to R-60 (RSI 5.3–10.6) in cold climates, walls to R-13 to R-21. Check your local energy code — it sets the legal minimum for new work.

What is the difference between R-value and RSI?

They measure the same thing — resistance to heat flow. R-value is the imperial unit; RSI is the metric unit used in Europe and Canada. RSI = R × 0.176, so R-20 is RSI 3.5 and a European 'R' printed on EU products is actually RSI in disguise.

Why does one bag cover less area at higher R?

Blown-in coverage charts are based on settled density: to reach a higher R in the same square metre you need more material depth, so each bag is spread thinner over fewer square metres. Always read the coverage row that matches your target R, not the headline figure.

How it works — the formula

Packs / bags = ceil( Area × (1 + Waste %) ÷ Coverage per pack/bag )
Thickness ≈ Target value ÷ Material value per unit thickness

R-values add up when layers are combined, and metric RSI converts as R = RSI × 5.678 (RSI = R × 0.176). Typical material performance: fibreglass about R-3.5 per inch (RSI 0.61 per 25 mm), mineral wool R-3.8/inch, blown cellulose R-3.3/inch, EPS board R-4.0/inch.

Worked example

Ceiling 10 m × 5 m = 50 m². Packs covering 5 m²: 50 ÷ 5 = 10 packs; with 10% extra → 11 packs. For fibreglass at RSI 3.5: thickness = 3.5 ÷ 0.61 × 25 mm ≈ 143 mm — so 150 mm batts, or two crossed layers of 75 mm.

Frequently asked questions

Should I add insulation in two layers?

For batts between joists, yes — a second layer laid across the joists at 90° covers the timber bridging and gaps of the first layer, and performs noticeably better than one thick layer fitted the same way.

Does compressing insulation reduce its R-value?

Yes. A batt squeezed into a shallower cavity than it was made for loses performance roughly in proportion to the compression. Choose a product made for your cavity depth instead of crushing a thicker one in.

How much extra should I allow?

10% covers cuts around joists, pipes and edges for batts and rolls. Blown-in settles a little after installation; installers usually blow 5–10% above the settled target depth, which the same allowance covers.
