🎨 Paint Calculator

Work out exactly how much paint and primer a room needs. Measure the room in metres or feet, tell the calculator how many doors and windows to subtract, how many coats you plan and the coverage printed on the tin — it does the rest, including a sensible extra allowance for spills and touch-ups.

How to use the Paint Calculator

  1. Choose your unit system — metric (metres) or imperial (feet).
  2. Enter the room length, width and wall height.
  3. Enter how many standard doors and windows to subtract.
  4. Set coats, the coverage from your paint tin, and primer coverage.
  5. Press Calculate — results include an extra allowance for waste.
Example: A 4 m × 3.5 m room with 2.7 m walls, 1 door and 1 window needs 36.8 m² of painting; at 2 coats and 10 m² per litre that is about 8.1 litres of paint plus 3.4 litres of primer (with 10% extra).

Frequently Asked Questions

How much paint do I need per square metre?

A typical interior emulsion covers about 10–12 m² per litre per coat on smooth walls. Two coats on 36.8 m² at 10 m²/L therefore needs about 7.4 litres before any waste allowance — this calculator adds the allowance for you.

Why does the calculator subtract doors and windows?

You do not paint over them, so leaving them in overstates the paint you need. Standard sizes (door 1.89 m², window 1.8 m²) are subtracted automatically; for very large windows or patio doors, increase the window count.

Do I really need primer?

On new plaster, bare drywall, stained walls or a strong colour change, yes — primer seals the surface so the topcoat covers evenly and you often save a third coat of expensive paint. On previously painted walls in good condition you can usually skip it.

How it works — the formula

The calculator first works out the paintable area, then divides by the coverage of your paint.

Wall area = 2 × (Length + Width) × Height − (Doors × 1.89 m²) − (Windows × 1.8 m²)
Paint needed = Wall area × Coats ÷ Coverage × (1 + Waste %)
Primer needed = Wall area ÷ Primer coverage × (1 + Waste %)

A standard interior door is taken as 0.9 m × 2.1 m (1.89 m², about 21 ft²) and a typical window as 1.5 m × 1.2 m (1.8 m², about 12 ft² of wall once the frame is counted). Imperial coverage in ft² per gallon is converted to m² per litre before calculating, so both systems give the same answer for the same room.

Worked example

Example: Room 4 m × 3.5 m, walls 2.7 m high, 1 door, 1 window, ceiling not painted. Wall area = 2 × (4 + 3.5) × 2.7 = 40.5 m², minus 1.89 + 1.8 = 3.69 m² → 36.81 m². Paint = 36.81 × 2 coats ÷ 10 m²/L × 1.10 = 8.10 litres. Primer = 36.81 ÷ 12 m²/L × 1.10 = 3.37 litres.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use this in feet and gallons?

Yes. Switch the unit system to Imperial: dimensions in feet, coverage in ft² per gallon, and results in gallons (with litres shown alongside). The answer matches the metric calculation for the same room.

How many coats should I plan?

Two coats is the standard for a durable, even finish. A similar colour over a sound surface may take one; a bold colour change or fresh plaster usually takes two over primer, sometimes three.

Why add a waste allowance?

Rollers and brushes hold paint, some is spilt, and you will want a little left for future touch-ups. 10% is a sensible default; use 15% for rough or textured walls.
