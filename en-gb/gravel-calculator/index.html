🪨 Gravel Calculator

Gravel, crushed stone, sand or soil for a drive, path or base layer: enter the area and the depth you want, and get the volume and the weight to order — in tonnes for metric bulk bags and deliveries, or cubic yards and US tons in imperial.

How to use the Gravel Calculator

  1. Choose metric or imperial units.
  2. Enter the length and width of the area.
  3. Enter the finished depth (50 mm / 2 in is typical over a base).
  4. Adjust the density if your material differs (1.5 t/m³ is typical gravel).
  5. Press Calculate for volume and weight, extra included.
Example: A 5 m × 2 m path at 50 mm deep needs 0.5 m³ of gravel — about 0.75 tonnes at the standard density of 1.5 t/m³.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much gravel do I need per square metre?

At 50 mm deep, one square metre needs 0.05 m³ — about 75 kg at the standard density of 1.5 t/m³. So a tonne covers roughly 13 m² at that depth, before any allowance for compaction.

What depth of gravel should I lay?

Over a compacted sub-base: 50 mm (2 in) for paths and drives with a decorative finish. On bare ground, plan a 100–150 mm sub-base layer plus 50 mm of gravel on top. Deeper than about 75 mm of loose gravel walks badly.

Why does the weight matter?

Loose materials are sold by weight (tonnes, bulk bags) as often as by volume. Suppliers deliver by the tonne, so the weight figure is the one you order — and the one your vehicle or barrow has to move.

How it works — the formula

Volume = Length × Width × Depth
Weight = Volume × (1 + Waste %) × Density

Typical loose densities: gravel and crushed stone 1.5 t/m³, sand 1.6 t/m³, topsoil 1.3–1.4 t/m³, bark mulch 0.3–0.4 t/m³. One cubic metre equals 1.308 cubic yards; one tonne equals 1.102 US tons.

Worked example

Example: Area 5 m × 2 m = 10 m², depth 0.05 m: Volume = 10 × 0.05 = 0.5 m³ (0.65 yd³). Weight = 0.5 × 1.5 = 0.75 tonnes before any extra allowance.

Frequently asked questions

What density should I use?

1.5 t/m³ for gravel and most crushed stone, 1.6 for sand, about 1.35 for topsoil, 1.7 for dense crushed concrete. If your supplier quotes a coverage figure, back-calculate: tonnes ordered ÷ volume placed gives your local density.

Does gravel compact down?

Yes — loose gravel and sub-base compact by roughly 10–15% once rolled or driven on. The default 10% extra allowance covers normal compaction; use 15% for sub-base layers.

Can I get the answer in cubic yards?

Yes — switch to Imperial for feet and inches; the result shows cubic yards and US tons alongside cubic metres and tonnes.
