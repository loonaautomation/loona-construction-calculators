☀️ Solar Panel Calculator

Thinking about rooftop solar: enter your monthly electricity use, your area's peak sun hours and the panel wattage you are considering, and get the number of panels, the system size in kW and the roof area it needs. (No unit switch here — electricity is the same everywhere; roof area is shown in both m² and ft².)

How to use the Solar Panel Calculator

  1. Find your monthly electricity use (kWh) on a recent bill — an annual average month is best.
  2. Enter your area's peak sun hours: roughly 3–4 in northern Europe, 4–5 in southern Europe and much of the US, 5–6.5 in the Middle East, South Asia and Australia.
  3. Enter the wattage of the panels you are considering (350–450 W is typical for residential panels).
  4. Keep the 15% system losses unless you have a reason to change them, then press Calculate.
Example: Using 900 kWh a month with 5 peak sun hours and 400 W panels, you need a 6 kW array in theory — 15 panels; allowing 15% losses, 18 panels (7.2 kW) is the realistic answer.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are peak sun hours for my area?

Rough averages: UK and northern Europe 2.5–3.5, central Europe 3–4, Mediterranean 4.5–5.5, US Sun Belt 5–6.5, Pakistan and northern India 5–6, Gulf states 5.5–6.5, most of Australia 4.5–6. Your installer or a solar irradiance map for your city will give a better figure.

How many panels fit on my roof?

A typical modern residential panel is about 1.9 m² (roughly 1.75 × 1.1 m). Measure the usable, unshaded roof slope, subtract space for ridges, chimneys and setbacks, and divide by 1.9 — a 40 m² slope fits around 20 panels.

Why include system losses?

Panels rarely deliver their lab rating in real life: the inverter converts at 95–98%, cables lose a little, and heat, dust and partial shading take more. 14–20% total is realistic; this calculator defaults to 15%. Ignoring losses is the most common reason home systems under-produce.

How it works — the formula

Daily use = Monthly kWh ÷ 30
Required system (kW) = Daily use ÷ (Peak sun hours × (1 − Losses %))
Panels = ceil( Required kW × 1000 ÷ Panel wattage )

One 'peak sun hour' is one hour of full-strength sun (1,000 W/m²) — a day's total sunshine expressed in those hours. Roof area assumes a typical residential panel footprint of about 1.9 m² (20 ft²).

Worked example

Use 900 kWh/month → daily use = 900 ÷ 30 = 30 kWh. Required = 30 ÷ (5 × 0.85) = 7.06 kW after 15% losses. Panels = 7,060 ÷ 400 = 17.6 → 18 panels, an actual array of 18 × 400 W = 7.2 kW needing about 34 m² of roof.

Frequently asked questions

Should my system cover 100% of my use?

Usually close to it, sized on annual rather than monthly production. In many places exported surplus is paid far less than imported electricity costs, so a system matched to your daytime use often pays back faster than the biggest system that fits.

Do I need batteries?

Not for the panels to work — a grid-tied system simply imports at night. Batteries add resilience during outages and let you use more of your own solar in the evening, but they are a separate cost decision; this calculator sizes the array only.

How long do solar panels last?

Most residential panels carry a 25-year performance warranty (typically 85–90% of original output at year 25) and degrade about 0.5% per year. Inverters usually need replacing once, around year 10–15.
