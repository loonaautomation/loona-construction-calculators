🛣️ Asphalt Calculator

Asphalt (tarmac) for a driveway, path or parking area: enter the area and the compacted thickness, and get the tonnage to order — tonnes in metric, US tons in imperial — using the standard hot-mix density, with an allowance for waste and an optional cost estimate.

How to use the Asphalt Calculator

  1. Choose metric or imperial units.
  2. Enter the length and width of the area to pave.
  3. Enter the finished (compacted) thickness — 50 mm / 2 in is typical for a driveway wearing course.
  4. Adjust the density if your mix differs (2,450 kg/m³ / 145 lb/ft³ is standard hot-mix).
  5. Press Calculate for the tonnage, and add a price to see the estimated cost.
Example: A 10 m × 4 m driveway at 50 mm compacted thickness needs 2 m³ of asphalt — about 4.9 tonnes at the standard density of 2,450 kg/m³.

Frequently Asked Questions

How thick should driveway asphalt be?

A common build-up is a 60–100 mm base (binder) course plus a 40–50 mm wearing course over a compacted sub-base. For a simple overlay on a sound existing surface, 50 mm compacted is typical. Heavy vehicles need more — check local guidance.

Why is asphalt sold by weight and not volume?

Plants batch asphalt by weight, and the laid weight per square metre is what determines whether you got the thickness you paid for. Weighbridge tickets also make deliveries easy to check: tonnes ÷ area ÷ density = average thickness.

What density should I use for asphalt?

2,450 kg/m³ (145 lb/ft³) is the standard figure for compacted hot-mix asphalt. Dense mixes with heavy aggregate can reach 2,500 kg/m³; open-graded or porous asphalt is lighter, around 2,300 kg/m³. Your supplier can confirm the mix density.

How it works — the formula

Volume = Length × Width × Thickness
Weight = Volume × (1 + Waste %) × Density

Compacted hot-mix asphalt weighs about 2,450 kg per cubic metre (145 lb per cubic foot) — noticeably denser than loose aggregate because the binder and compaction leave almost no air voids. One tonne equals 1.102 US tons; one cubic metre equals 1.308 cubic yards.

Worked example

Area 10 m × 4 m = 40 m², thickness 0.05 m: Volume = 40 × 0.05 = 2 m³. Weight = 2 × 2,450 kg/m³ = 4,900 kg = 4.9 tonnes (5.40 US tons) before any waste allowance.

Frequently asked questions

How much extra should I order?

The default 10% covers uneven sub-grade, edges, joints and the material that stays in the truck and tools. On a perfectly prepared, simple rectangle, 5% may be enough; on irregular shapes use 10–15%. Running short mid-lay leaves a cold joint, so err on the generous side.

Does loose asphalt weigh the same as compacted?

The weight is the same material, but loose asphalt occupies more volume — it compacts by roughly 20–25% when rolled. Always calculate from the finished, compacted thickness, which is what this calculator asks for.

Can I use this for a pothole or patch?

Yes — enter the patch length, width and depth. For small repairs sold in bags, divide the tonnage by the bag weight (typically 25 kg) to get the bag count.
