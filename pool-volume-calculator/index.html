🏊 Pool Volume Calculator

How much water is in your pool? Pick the shape — rectangle, round or oval — enter the size and the shallow and deep end depths, and get the volume in litres and US gallons: the number you need for chemical dosing, heating and filling time.

How to use the Pool Volume Calculator

  1. Choose metric or imperial units.
  2. Pick the pool shape: rectangle, round or oval.
  3. Enter the length and width (for a round pool the width is the diameter).
  4. Enter the shallow-end and deep-end water depths — the calculator averages them, which suits a straight sloped floor.
  5. Press Calculate for litres and US gallons.
Example: An 8 m × 4 m pool with a flat 1.5 m depth holds 48 m³ — 48,000 litres, about 12,680 US gallons.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I measure my pool if it has a sloped floor?

Measure the water depth at the shallow end and at the deep end (to the waterline, not the top of the wall) and enter both — the calculator averages them. For a pool with a steep 'hopper' bottom, the true volume is a little less than the average suggests; dose chemicals conservatively and adjust by testing.

Why do I need the volume in litres or gallons?

Almost every pool chemical — chlorine, pH adjusters, algaecide, flocculant — is dosed per volume of water. Guessing the volume is the main reason pools end up over- or under-dosed; a measured volume makes test results and doses line up.

How long will it take to fill my pool?

Divide the volume by your hose or tap flow. A typical garden hose delivers 15–20 litres per minute: 48,000 litres ÷ 18 L/min ≈ 2,670 minutes — about 44 hours. Most people fill in stages over two days.

How it works — the formula

Average depth = (Shallow depth + Deep depth) ÷ 2
Rectangle: Volume = Length × Width × Average depth
Round: Volume = π × (Diameter ÷ 2)² × Average depth
Oval: Volume = π × (Length ÷ 2) × (Width ÷ 2) × Average depth

One cubic metre is exactly 1,000 litres and about 264.2 US gallons. The average-depth method assumes the floor slopes evenly from shallow to deep end, which is true of most domestic pools.

Worked example

Rectangle pool 8 m × 4 m, depth a flat 1.5 m both ends: average depth = 1.5 m. Volume = 8 × 4 × 1.5 = 48 m³ = 48,000 litres = 48,000 ÷ 3.785 ≈ 12,680 US gallons.

Frequently asked questions

Does a kidney or freeform pool fit these shapes?

Approximately. Use the oval formula with the longest length and widest width, then reduce the result by about 10–15% for the curves that cut the corners. It is close enough for dosing, especially if you confirm with your first chemical test.

How much water does a round above-ground pool hold?

Enter the diameter as the width and the wall height minus about 15 cm (the water sits below the rim) as both depths. A 4.6 m (15 ft) pool filled to 1.1 m holds about 18,300 litres (4,830 US gallons).

Should I use US or imperial gallons?

US gallons (3.785 L) — used by virtually all pool product labels. An imperial gallon is 4.546 L; if an old British product doses per imperial gallon, convert your litres first (litres ÷ 4.546).
