💷 Electricity Cost Calculator

What does that heater, pump or air conditioner actually cost to run? Enter its wattage (or its amps and volts), how long it runs each day and your price per kWh, and get the energy used and the cost per day, month and year — in any currency.

How to use the Electricity Cost Calculator

  1. Find the appliance's power: the watts on its rating label, or its amps — fill in amps and volts instead and the calculator multiplies them.
  2. Enter realistic hours per day and days per month (a heater used only on weekdays might be 22 days, not 30).
  3. Take the price per kWh from your latest bill — divide the bill total by the kWh used if your tariff is not shown directly — then press Calculate.
Example: A 1,500 W heater run 4 hours a day, 30 days a month, at 0.30 per kWh uses 180 kWh a month and costs 54.00 a month.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where do I find my price per kWh?

On your electricity bill: either a stated unit rate, or divide the total energy charge by the kWh consumed. Include only the per-unit energy charges if you want the pure running cost; standing charges and taxes apply whether or not this appliance runs.

The label shows amps, not watts — what do I do?

Fill in the amps and your supply voltage (230 V in most of Europe and Pakistan, 120 V in North America) and leave the calculator to multiply them: W = A × V. If you fill in both amps and volts, those override the watts box.

Why is my real bill lower than the estimate for a fridge or air conditioner?

Those appliances cycle: the compressor or thermostat switches the load on and off, so average draw is often 30–70% of the rating. Heaters, kettles, pumps and lights, by contrast, draw close to their rated watts whenever they are on, so the estimate is close for them.

How it works — the formula

Power from amps: W = A × V
Energy per day (kWh) = Watts ÷ 1,000 × Hours per day
Energy per month = kWh per day × Days per month  ·  Energy per year = kWh per month × 12
Cost = kWh × Price per kWh

One kilowatt-hour is one kilowatt running for one hour — it is the unit your bill is counted in. This is a generic appliance running-cost tool for any country and currency. For a full household bill in Pakistan, including the slab rates, taxes and surcharges that apply there, use the dedicated Pakistan Electricity Bill Calculator on Loona Calculators instead — a different tool for a different job.

Worked example

Heater: 1,500 W, 4 h/day, 30 days/month, price 0.30 per kWh. Energy per day = 1,500 ÷ 1,000 × 4 = 6 kWh. Per month = 6 × 30 = 180 kWh. Cost per month = 180 × 0.30 = 54.00; per day = 6 × 0.30 = 1.80; per year = 180 × 12 = 2,160 kWh costing 648.00.

Frequently asked questions

How do I cost a whole house or workshop?

Cost the big loads one at a time — heating, cooling, water heating, pumps and lighting are usually 80–90% of a bill — and add the results. Small always-on loads (routers, standby TVs) are cheap individually but run 8,760 hours a year, so check them too.

Does this work for any country and currency?

Yes. The maths is the same everywhere, and the results are in whatever currency your price per kWh is in — dollars, pounds, euros, rupees. (For Pakistan's full slab-based household bill, use the Loona Calculators electricity bill tool linked in the formula section.)

What is a kWh in plain terms?

Running a 1,000 W appliance for 1 hour uses 1 kWh. A 100 W bulb takes 10 hours to use the same 1 kWh — which is why wattage × hours, not wattage alone, decides the cost.
