🪜 Stairs Calculator

Designing a staircase or deck steps? Enter the total floor-to-floor rise and your tread depth, and the calculator chooses a step count, works out the exact riser height so every step is identical, the total run, and the stringer length — and checks the result against the classic comfort rule.

How to use the Stairs Calculator

  1. Choose metric or imperial units.
  2. Enter the total rise — floor to floor, including the finished floor thickness.
  3. Enter your tread depth (250 mm / 10 in is a comfortable default).
  4. Press Calculate: the step count is chosen so every riser is exactly equal.
Example: A 2.7 m floor-to-floor rise works out to 15 risers of exactly 180 mm, 14 treads, a 3.5 m run at 250 mm treads, and a stringer of about 4.42 m — comfort value 610 mm, inside the ideal range.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the ideal riser height and tread depth?

For homes, risers of 170–190 mm (about 7 in) with treads (goings) of 250–300 mm (10–12 in) feel natural. The exact combination matters less than the comfort rule: 2 × riser + tread ≈ 630 mm.

Why must every riser be exactly the same?

People climb stairs by rhythm, not by looking. A riser that differs by even 10 mm from the rest is a classic trip hazard — which is why the calculator divides the total rise into perfectly equal steps instead of using a fixed riser and a leftover.

How do I measure the total rise?

From the finished floor level downstairs to the finished floor level upstairs — including flooring thickness at both ends. Measure twice at the stairwell opening; an error here spreads across every step.

How it works — the formula

Number of risers = Total rise ÷ Target riser (175 mm / 7 in), rounded to a whole number
Actual riser = Total rise ÷ Number of risers (identical for every step — this is the key to a safe stair)
Total run = Tread depth × (Risers − 1)
Stringer length = √(Total rise² + Total run²)
Comfort rule: 2 × Riser + Tread should be about 600–650 mm (24–25.5 in) — the length of a natural stride on stairs.

Worked example

Example: Total rise 2700 mm ÷ 175 = 15.4 → 15 risers of 2700 ÷ 15 = 180 mm each. Treads = 14 × 250 mm = 3500 mm run. Stringer = √(2700² + 3500²) = 4420 mm. Comfort: 2 × 180 + 250 = 610 mm — inside the 600–650 mm range.

Frequently asked questions

What is the stringer length used for?

The stringer is the diagonal board (or steel section) that carries the treads. Its minimum length is the hypotenuse of rise and run; order longer than the calculated figure to allow for the end cuts and the connection details.

How much headroom do I need?

Building rules typically require at least 2 m (about 6 ft 8 in) of clear headroom above the stair line, measured vertically from the tread nosings. Check the stairwell opening length when you fix the run.

Are these building-regulation stairs?

The calculator uses widely accepted design targets (riser ≈175 mm, comfort 600–650 mm), but maximum riser and minimum going limits differ by country and building type. Always confirm your design against your local building code before building.
