🏠 Roof Pitch Calculator

Enter a roof's rise and run — in metres, feet, centimetres or inches, the ratio is the same — and get everything at once: the pitch ratio in x-in-12 form, the slope angle in degrees, the grade as a percentage, and the rafter (slope) length for your run.

How to use the Roof Pitch Calculator

  1. Enter the rise — how much the roof climbs vertically.
  2. Enter the run — the horizontal distance over which it climbs.
  3. Use the same unit for both (feet, metres, inches — it does not matter).
  4. Press Calculate for pitch ratio, angle, grade and rafter length.
Example: A roof that rises 3 over a run of 12 has a 3-in-12 pitch: a 14.04° angle, a 25% grade, and a rafter length of 12.37 for every 12 of run.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a 4-in-12 roof pitch in degrees?

About 18.4°. Common conversions: 3-in-12 = 14.0°, 6-in-12 = 26.6°, 9-in-12 = 36.9°, 12-in-12 = 45°. The angle is the arctangent of rise ÷ run.

What is the minimum pitch for roof tiles?

It depends on the covering: standard concrete tiles typically need at least 22.5°, plain tiles 35°, slates 25–30°, while metal sheeting and membranes can go much lower. Always check the manufacturer's limit for your exact product.

How do I measure my roof's pitch?

From inside the attic or off a ladder at the gable: hold a level 12 inches (or 30 cm) horizontally from the roof surface and measure the vertical distance down to the roof — that is your rise per 12 (or per 30 cm, scaled).

How it works — the formula

Pitch (x-in-12) = Rise ÷ Run × 12
Angle = arctan(Rise ÷ Run), in degrees
Grade = Rise ÷ Run × 100%
Rafter length = √(Rise² + Run²) — Pythagoras on the slope triangle

North American roofing expresses pitch as inches of rise per 12 inches of run; most of the rest of the world uses the angle or the percentage. This calculator shows all three.

Worked example

Example: Rise 3, run 12: pitch = 3 ÷ 12 × 12 = 3-in-12. Angle = arctan(0.25) = 14.04°. Grade = 25%. Rafter = √(3² + 12²) = √153 = 12.37 per 12 of run.

Frequently asked questions

Does the unit matter — metres or inches?

No. Pitch is a ratio of two lengths in the same unit, so metres, feet, centimetres or inches all give the same pitch, angle and grade. Only the rafter length follows the unit you entered.

What is a steep roof pitch?

Above about 9-in-12 (36.9°) is considered steep — harder and slower to work on. 4-in-12 to 9-in-12 is the conventional range for houses; below 4-in-12 is low-slope and needs coverings rated for it.

How long a rafter do I order?

Take the rafter length for your full run, then add the eaves overhang (measured along the slope) and a little extra for the ridge cut and trimming. This calculator gives the pure slope length between the two measured points.
