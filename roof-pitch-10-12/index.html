10/12 Roof Pitch — 39.8°

a 10/12 pitch is 39.81°, grade 83%, rafter multiplier 1.3017 — with rafter tables and material guidance. Everything for one pitch: angle, grade, the rafter multiplier, lengths for common runs, and honest notes on what roofing belongs on a 10/12 slope.

10/12 pitch at a glance

Pitch10 in 12 (rise 10 units per 12 units of run)
Slope angle39.81°
Grade83.3%
Rafter multiplier1.3017 — multiply the horizontal run by this to get rafter (slope) length
Rafter length per 12 ft run15.62 ft
Typical usesMountain homes, churches, historic styles

Slope length only — add eaves overhang and ridge allowance when ordering timber, exactly as the Roof Pitch Calculator notes.

Working with a 10/12 pitch

Steep: heavy snow country and classic European roofs live here. Every extra degree is labour cost, but the roof sheds almost anything.

Worked example

A garage roof spans 24 ft, so each side runs 12 ft horizontally. At 10/12 the ridge rises 10 ft above the wall plate, and each rafter is 12 × 1.3017 = 15.62 ft of slope length before overhangs. Roofing material is ordered on slope area: the 24 × 30 ft building (two 12-ft runs, 30 ft long) needs 2 × 15.62 × 30 ≈ 937 sq ft of covering — not the 720 sq ft of the floor plan. That multiplier gap is where DIY budgets go wrong.

Frequently Asked Questions

What angle is a 10/12 pitch?

39.81 degrees from horizontal — a 83% grade.

How long is a rafter at 10/12?

Run × 1.3017. A 12 ft run needs 15.62 ft of rafter before overhang and ridge allowances.

Is 10/12 walkable?

Roofers generally treat up to about 6/12 as walkable with care, 7/12–9/12 as work for jacks and planks, and 10/12+ as scaffold territory. Local safety rules always win — this page describes pitch, not a safety method.
