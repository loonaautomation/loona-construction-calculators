🚧 Fence Calculator

Building a picket or rail fence: enter the total fence length, post spacing and your picket size, and get the full shopping list — posts, rails, pickets and the concrete bags to set the posts — in metric or imperial.

How to use the Fence Calculator

  1. Choose metric or imperial units.
  2. Enter the total fence length and the post spacing you plan (2.4–3 m / 6–8 ft is typical).
  3. Enter the number of horizontal rails per section.
  4. Enter your picket width and the gap you want between pickets.
  5. Set the concrete bags per post for your soil and bag size, then press Calculate.
Example: A 30 m fence with 3 m post spacing needs 10 sections and 11 posts; with 2 rails per section that is 20 rails, and 100 mm pickets at a 10 mm gap need about 273 pickets.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far apart should fence posts be?

For most garden fences 1.8–3 m (6–10 ft) apart; 2.4 m (8 ft) matches standard rail and panel sizes. Closer spacing (1.8 m) is sturdier for tall or solid fences in windy spots; wider spacing suits open ranch-style rails.

How deep should fence posts go?

A common rule is one third of the post in the ground, with a minimum of about 600 mm (2 ft) for garden fences. In frost-prone areas the hole should reach below the frost line so the post is not lifted in winter.

How many concrete bags does one post really need?

It depends on hole size and bag size. A 300 mm (12 in) diameter, 600 mm deep hole takes roughly 2 bags of 25 kg (or 50 lb) post-mix per post. Bigger holes or softer ground need more — the field lets you set your own figure.

How it works — the formula

Sections = ceil(Fence length ÷ Post spacing); Posts = Sections + 1
Rails = Sections × Rails per section
Pickets = ceil( Fence length ÷ (Picket width + Gap) × (1 + Waste %) )

Posts are counted for a straight run with a post at each end. Corners, ends of runs and gate openings each need their own post — add them on top of this count.

Worked example

Fence 30 m, spacing 3 m: sections = 30 ÷ 3 = 10, posts = 10 + 1 = 11. Rails = 10 × 2 = 20 pieces. Pickets: pitch = 100 + 10 = 110 mm; 30 m ÷ 0.11 m = 272.7 → 273 pickets. Concrete at 2 bags per post = 22 bags.

Frequently asked questions

Should pickets touch each other?

No — leave a small gap (6–10 mm typical). Timber swells when wet, and a gap also lets wind pass, reducing the load on the fence. A spacer block cut to your gap width keeps spacing consistent as you fix.

Do I need gravel in the post hole?

A 50–100 mm layer of gravel at the bottom of the hole helps drainage and slows rot at the post end, even when the rest of the hole is filled with concrete.

What about gates and corners?

Each corner and each side of a gate needs its own (usually heavier) post, and a gate opening breaks the picket run. Measure gate widths out of the total length if you want an exact picket count.
