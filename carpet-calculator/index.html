🧶 Carpet Calculator

Carpet is sold off a roll, so the area you pay for is rarely the area of your room. Enter the room, the roll width and your waste allowance, and get the linear length to order and the true purchase area — in metric or imperial, with an optional cost.

How to use the Carpet Calculator

  1. Choose metric or imperial units.
  2. Enter the room length and width (measure into doorways and alcoves).
  3. Enter the roll width your carpet comes on — commonly 4 or 5 m, or 12 (sometimes 13.5) ft.
  4. Add your waste allowance (10% is standard) and a price if you want a cost estimate, then press Calculate.
Example: A 5 m × 4 m room carpeted from a 4 m roll needs one 5 m strip — 5.5 m with 10% extra, so you buy 22 m² of carpet for a 20 m² room.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do I pay for more carpet than my room's area?

Because carpet comes in fixed roll widths. If your room is 4.6 m wide and the roll is 4 m, you need two strips: one full width and one cut from a second length — and you pay for both full strips. Choosing a roll width close to your room width minimises this.

Which direction should the carpet run?

Pile direction should be consistent across joins, and ideally the light from windows should not shine across a seam. Within those limits, run the roll along whichever room dimension needs fewer strips — enter both orientations in the calculator and compare the purchase area.

How much waste should I allow for carpet?

10% is the standard allowance for a plain carpet in a rectangular room. Use 15% or more for patterned carpet that must match at the seams, rooms with alcoves and bay windows, or stairs.

How it works — the formula

Strips per room = ceil(Room width ÷ Roll width)
Linear length = Strips × Room length × (1 + Waste %)
Area you pay for = Linear length × Roll width

Carpet pricing per square metre can mislead: you pay for the full roll width across every strip, including the off-cut. Rooms narrower than the roll are cheapest — one strip, no joins.

Worked example

Room 5 m × 4 m, roll 4 m wide: strips = ceil(4 ÷ 4) = 1. Linear = 1 × 5 m × 1.10 = 5.5 m. Area paid = 5.5 × 4 = 22 m², for a room of 20 m².

Frequently asked questions

Do I need underlay and grippers in this estimate?

No — this calculator covers the carpet itself. Underlay is sold by room area (the true room area shown in the results, not the purchase area), and gripper rods by the room perimeter.

Can one roll length serve two rooms?

Often yes: if two rooms are carpeted in the same carpet, a single long roll length can sometimes be cut to serve both, reducing off-cut waste. Fitters plan this on the day; the per-room figure here is the safe ordering estimate.

What roll widths are standard?

In Europe 4 m and 5 m dominate; in the US 12 ft is standard with some 13.5 and 15 ft. Broadloom wider than your room avoids seams entirely — worth checking before accepting a two-strip quote.
