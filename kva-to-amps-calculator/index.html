🔷 kVA to Amps Calculator

Generators, transformers and UPS units are rated in kVA — but breakers and cables work in amps. Convert either way: kVA + voltage gives the full-load current, or amps + voltage gives the kVA a load needs.

How to use the kVA to Amps Calculator

  1. Pick the direction and the supply type (single-phase or three-phase).
  2. Fill in the kVA (or the amps, for the reverse direction) and the voltage — line-to-line for three-phase.
  3. Press Calculate for the converted value.
Example: A 50 kVA three-phase generator at 400 V delivers 50,000 ÷ (√3 × 400) = 72.17 A per line at full load.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are generators and transformers rated in kVA, not kW?

Because their limit is current and voltage — the heating in their windings depends on amps, not on how much of that current does useful work. A 50 kVA generator can supply 50 kW to a resistive load (PF 1) but only 40 kW to a load at PF 0.8, at the same 72 A on a 400 V three-phase output.

How do I get kW from kVA?

Multiply by the power factor: kW = kVA × PF. At the common generator rating assumption of PF 0.8, 50 kVA = 40 kW. The Power Factor Calculator works through the full triangle.

Which voltage do I use for three-phase?

The line-to-line voltage — the one in the system's name: 400 V for a 400/230 V system, 480 V, 208 V or 600 V in North America. Do not use the 230 V line-to-neutral figure in the three-phase formula.

How it works — the formula

Single-phase: I = kVA × 1,000 ÷ V  ·  kVA = V × I ÷ 1,000
Three-phase: I = kVA × 1,000 ÷ (√3 × VL-L)  ·  kVA = √3 × VL-L × I ÷ 1,000

kVA (apparent power) is voltage × current with no power-factor adjustment — that is why generators and transformers are rated in it: they must supply the current whatever the load's power factor. Real power in kW = kVA × PF.

Worked example

Three-phase, S = 50 kVA, V = 400 V: I = 50 × 1,000 ÷ (√3 × 400) = 50,000 ÷ 692.82 = 72.17 A. Single-phase comparison: a 10 kVA supply at 230 V gives 10,000 ÷ 230 = 43.48 A.

Frequently asked questions

How many amps is 1 kVA?

At 400 V three-phase, 1 kVA = 1.44 A. At 230 V single-phase, 1 kVA = 4.35 A. At 120 V single-phase, 8.33 A. Always state the voltage with any kVA-to-amps figure.

Can this size my generator for a motor?

It gives the running current. Motors draw 6–8× their running current while starting, so generator sizing for motor loads needs the starting kVA checked too — a common rule is that the generator's kVA should comfortably exceed the motor's starting demand or a soft starter should be fitted.

What is the difference between kVA and VA?

Just scale: 1 kVA = 1,000 VA. Small UPS units are often rated in VA (e.g. 1,500 VA = 1.5 kVA); the formulas here work if you enter VA ÷ 1,000 as kVA.
