Paint Needed for 100 Sq Ft of Wall

two coats over 100 sq ft of wall take about 2.0 L of paint at typical coverage — primer and one-coat figures included. Straight answers for a 100-sq-ft wall: litres and gallons for one coat, two coats and primer, with the coverage assumption printed on the tin of every number.

Paint for 100 sq ft of wall area

Wall area100 sq ft (9.3 m²)
Coverage assumed10 m² per litre per coat (typical emulsion mid-range; tins quote 10–12)
Paint — 2 coats, incl. 10% extra2.0 L (0.5 US gal)
Paint — 1 coat1.0 L (0.3 gal)
Primer — 1 coat @ 12 m²/L, incl. extra0.9 L (0.2 gal)
Typical buy1 × 10 L tubs or 1 × 1 gal cans (rounded up)

Wall area ≠ floor area. This page is for 100 sq ft of actual wall. For a square room, wall area ≈ 4 × √(floor area) × ceiling height — a 100 sq ft room with 8 ft ceilings has ≈290 sq ft of wall. The Paint Calculator works from room dimensions and subtracts doors and windows properly.

Worked example

At 10 m²/L, one coat over 9 m² drinks 0.9 L; two coats with the standard 10% extra land at the table's 2.0 L. Rough or previously unpainted plaster can drop coverage to 7–8 m²/L — if your surface is thirsty, buy toward the calculator's conservative side rather than this page's mid-range figure.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much paint covers 100 sq ft?

Two coats at typical coverage: 2.0 litres (0.5 gallons) including 10% extra; one coat is half that. Primer adds 0.9 L.

Why do brands quote different coverage?

Solids content and surface porosity. The 10 m²/L used here is the honest middle of the 8–12 m²/L that major emulsions quote — your tin's figure wins; type it into the Paint Calculator for an exact answer.

Is this wall or floor area?

Wall. Confusing the two is the classic under-buy: walls are typically about 3× the floor area of the same room once ceiling height counts.

Do I need primer?

On new plaster, patched areas and strong colour changes, yes — one coat at the table's quantity. On sound, previously painted walls in a similar colour, usually not.
