🔲 Tile Calculator

Find out how many tiles — and how many boxes — a floor or wall really takes. Enter the area, the tile size and the grout gap in centimetres/millimetres or inches; the calculator counts a tile plus its grout joint for every position and adds an allowance for cuts and breakages.

How to use the Tile Calculator

  1. Choose metric or imperial units.
  2. Enter the length and width of the floor or wall.
  3. Enter the tile size and the grout gap you plan (3 mm / ⅛ in is typical).
  4. Enter how many tiles come in one box.
  5. Press Calculate for tiles and boxes, including the waste allowance.
Example: A 4 m × 3 m floor with 60 × 60 cm tiles needs 37 tiles including 10% extra — 10 boxes when tiles come 4 to a box.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many 60 × 60 tiles are in a square metre?

A 600 × 600 mm tile covers 0.36 m², so one square metre needs 2.78 tiles before waste — about 3.06 tiles with a 10% allowance, or 34 tiles for a 12 m² floor before rounding to whole tiles.

Why does the grout gap change the count?

Each tile is laid with a grout joint on two sides, so the floor space one tile actually covers is (tile + gap) squared. With small tiles and wide joints the difference is significant; with large-format tiles it is small but real.

How much extra should I allow for cuts?

10% is the standard allowance for straight layouts in rectangular rooms. Use 15% for diagonal (45°) layouts, patterned tiles or rooms with many corners and obstacles.

How it works — the formula

Tiles are laid on a grid where each tile occupies its own size plus one grout joint — the pitch.

Area = Length × Width
Pitch area = (Tile length + Gap) × (Tile width + Gap)
Tiles = Area × (1 + Waste %) ÷ Pitch area, rounded up
Boxes = Tiles ÷ Tiles per box, rounded up

Worked example

Example: Floor 4 m × 3 m = 12 m². Tiles 600 × 600 mm with no extra gap allowance: pitch area = 0.6 × 0.6 = 0.36 m². Tiles = 12 × 1.10 ÷ 0.36 = 36.7 → 37 tiles. At 4 per box: 37 ÷ 4 = 9.25 → 10 boxes.

Frequently asked questions

Can I calculate wall tiles the same way?

Yes — enter the wall length and its height as the two dimensions. For a whole bathroom, add the walls together or run the calculator once per wall and add the boxes.

Why buy a spare box?

Tiles are made in batches (dye lots) and shades vary slightly between them. A spare box from the same batch makes future repairs invisible; a later box may not match.

Does it work in inches and feet?

Yes — switch to Imperial for dimensions in feet, tile sizes and grout gaps in inches. The tile count for the same job matches the metric result.
