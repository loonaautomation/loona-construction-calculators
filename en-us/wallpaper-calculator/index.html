🧻 Wallpaper Calculator

How many rolls of wallpaper does a room take? Enter the room, choose a European roll (0.53 × 10.05 m) or a US double roll, and add your pattern repeat — the calculator works in strips, exactly like a decorator, so repeats and trimming are properly allowed for.

How to use the Wallpaper Calculator

  1. Choose metric or imperial units.
  2. Enter the room length, width and wall height, minus doors and windows by count.
  3. Choose the roll type — European or US double roll.
  4. Enter the pattern repeat from the label (0 for plain or random-match paper).
  5. Press Calculate for the number of rolls.
Example: A 4 m × 3.5 m room with 2.5 m walls, no deductions, European rolls and no pattern repeat takes 11 rolls including 10% extra.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many rolls of wallpaper do I need for a room?

Count in strips: divide the roll length by your strip length (wall height + trimming, rounded up to the pattern repeat) to get strips per roll, then divide the wall area by the usable area of one roll. A typical 4 × 3.5 m room takes about 10–11 European rolls.

What is pattern repeat and why does it cost rolls?

The repeat is the distance before the pattern matches again (printed on the label, e.g. 53 cm). Every strip must start at a matching point, so each strip is rounded up to a whole number of repeats — the offcut is waste. Large repeats on tall walls can add a roll or two.

European roll or US roll — what is the difference?

The European standard roll is 0.53 m wide × 10.05 m long (about 5.3 m²). US papers are sold as single or double rolls; a double roll is typically 27 in × 27 ft. This calculator offers the European roll and the US double roll.

How it works — the formula

Decorators count wallpaper in strips, not square meters:

Strip length = Wall height + 10 cm trimming, rounded up to a whole number of pattern repeats
Strips per roll = Roll length ÷ Strip length (whole strips only)
Usable area per roll = Roll width × Strips per roll × Wall height
Rolls = Net wall area × (1 + Waste %) ÷ Usable area per roll, rounded up

Net wall area subtracts standard doors (1.89 m²) and windows (1.8 m²) from the full wall area.

Worked example

Example: Room 4 m × 3.5 m, height 2.5 m, no doors or windows deducted: net area = 2 × 7.5 × 2.5 = 37.5 m². Strip length = 2.6 m; a 10.05 m European roll gives ⌊10.05 ÷ 2.6⌋ = 3 strips; usable per roll = 0.53 × 3 × 2.5 = 3.975 m². Rolls = 37.5 × 1.10 ÷ 3.975 = 10.4 → 11 rolls.

Frequently asked questions

Should I subtract doors and windows?

Yes for wallpaper — unlike plasterboard, offcuts above doors are short and genuinely reduce what you hang, so standard doors (1.89 m²) and windows (1.8 m²) are deducted from the wall area here.

Why buy an extra roll?

The 10% allowance covers mis-cuts and the strip that starts a new wall. Beyond that, always buy every roll in one order from the same batch — dye lots differ visibly, and a later top-up roll may not match.

Can I use it in feet?

Yes — switch to Imperial for feet and inches (pattern repeat in inches). Roll definitions stay the physical European and US standards, so counts are directly comparable.
