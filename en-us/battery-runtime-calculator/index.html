🔋 Battery Runtime Calculator

Planning a solar battery, a UPS or an off-grid setup? Enter the battery's voltage and amp-hour capacity, the load in watts, how deeply you are willing to discharge it and the inverter efficiency — and get the usable energy and the runtime to expect.

How to use the Battery Runtime Calculator

  1. Enter the battery voltage and its amp-hour (Ah) rating — for a bank of batteries, use the bank's total voltage and Ah at that voltage.
  2. Enter the load in watts (add up everything that will run at once).
  3. Set the depth of discharge you will actually use and the inverter efficiency, then press Calculate.
Example: A 12 V, 100 Ah battery running a 100 W load at 50% depth of discharge through an 85%-efficient inverter: usable energy = 510 Wh, runtime = 5.1 hours.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why not just divide Ah by the load amps?

Because the load is usually in watts on the AC side, and two real-world factors intervene: the depth of discharge you should limit yourself to, and the inverter's losses. Ignoring both overstates runtime by 40% or more on a lead-acid system.

What depth of discharge should I use?

Lead-acid (including AGM and gel): 50% for a reasonable service life. LiFePO4 lithium: 80–90% is routinely used. Check your battery maker's datasheet — cycle life is quoted at a stated DoD, and deeper cycling shortens life sharply.

How do I combine several batteries?

In series, voltages add and Ah stays the same (two 12 V 100 Ah in series = 24 V 100 Ah). In parallel, Ah adds and voltage stays (two in parallel = 12 V 200 Ah). Either way the total watt-hours are the same — enter the bank's combined volts and Ah.

How it works — the formula

Stored energy (Wh) = Volts × Amp-hours
Usable energy (Wh) = Stored × Depth of discharge × Efficiency
Runtime (hours) = Usable Wh ÷ Load (W)
Average battery current (A) = Load ÷ (Volts × Efficiency)

Two honest adjustments separate this from the naive 'Ah ÷ amps' sums: you should not empty most batteries (a lead-acid battery regularly discharged past 50% wears out fast, while LiFePO4 tolerates 80–90%), and an inverter wastes 10–20% of the energy it converts. Both are in the formula above.

Worked example

12 V × 100 Ah = 1,200 Wh stored. Usable = 1,200 × 0.50 × 0.85 = 510 Wh. Runtime = 510 ÷ 100 W = 5.1 hours (5 h 6 min). The battery supplies on average 100 ÷ (12 × 0.85) = 9.80 A while doing it.

Frequently asked questions

Why did my real runtime come out shorter?

Common reasons: the battery's Ah rating is measured at a slow 20-hour discharge — draw faster and lead-acid delivers less (Peukert effect); cold batteries hold less; old batteries fade; and inverter standby draw adds to the load. Treat the result as a best case.

Can I run the load straight from DC and skip the inverter loss?

If the load accepts the battery voltage (lights, many 12 V appliances), set efficiency to 100% — actually 95–98% to allow for wiring — and the runtime rises accordingly. Never connect an AC-only appliance directly to a battery.
