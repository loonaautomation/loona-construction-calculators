Concrete Needed for 2000 Sq Ft

a 2000 sq ft slab at 4 in thick needs 20.77 m³ (27.16 yd³) of concrete with 10% extra — or the bag counts in the table. The exact order quantities for a 2000 sq ft pour — ready-mix volume and pre-mix bags, computed with the same yields as the site's Concrete Calculator.

The figures use exactly the Loona Concrete Calculator's model — volume = area × thickness, bags at the manufacturers' standard yields (an 80 lb bag makes ≈0.017 m³ / 0.6 ft³). For a different thickness or waste allowance, the Concrete Calculator recomputes instantly.

Worked example — ordering ready-mix

Suppliers sell by the cubic yard/metre and round to their minimum load. For 2000 sq ft you order 27.16 yd³ — call it 27.5 yd³ with most suppliers' half-yard rounding, and confirm the short-load fee before the truck rolls. Bagging this slab instead means 1,222 80-lb bags — beyond about 1 yd³, ready-mix is almost always cheaper and always faster.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much concrete do I need for 2000 sq ft?

At 4 inches thick: 18.88 m³ exact, 20.77 m³ (27.16 yd³) with the customary 10% extra for spillage, uneven subgrade and over-excavation.

What if my slab is 6 inches thick?

Multiply every volume and bag figure here by 1.5 — volume scales linearly with thickness. The Concrete Calculator handles any thickness directly.

Bags or ready-mix?

This page lists both. As a rule of thumb, under ~0.5 yd³ bags are convenient; at 27.2 yd³ a truck is the sane choice for most people.

Does the area shape matter?

Only through thickness and waste. Volume is area × thickness whatever the shape — irregular shapes just deserve the full 10% extra.
